OneThe principles we hold

Four, drawn from both authentic relating and circling. Spoken between the two of us rather than read out, with a live example each. Not a final exam, an offering.

Welcome everything, trust your experience

Body awareness is the anchor

Welcoming everything means not trying to change your experience into something else. Opening your perception, observing what is, without judgment, with curiosity. Giving it space instead of shoving parts of it away because they feel wrong to have.

Some parts of our experience we were conditioned not to welcome. If someone grew up having to hide anger, or ignore it, contact with that anger gets lost, and welcoming it back is harder. That is often exactly where the growth is, and curiosity is what makes it possible.

Alongside welcoming there is trusting. It can be hard to trust shame, or discomfort, and to let it have room inside. Okay, I am feeling it right now, that is what is present. Trusting that is where the magic happens. What we try to bring is a space where that is allowed.

Whenever it gets abstract, the body brings you back. The anchor is always the sensation, the breath, the ground.

Ways to say it in the room
Nothing that arises here is a mistake. You do not have to make it into something better before you bring it. If you lose the thread, come back to your breath and to the floor under you.

Present moment focus, and not going into stories

Sensation, feeling, story

The first default for most of us is the story and the explanation. Here we offer dropping into the present instead. The easiest door is the body: what the breath is like, how it is to be in this body right now, whether there is an impulse to move away from someone or toward them, and simply being with that.

It is precious to be able to tell these three apart, because it changes what you can bring into the space.

SensationMy stomach is growling. My cheeks are hot. Something is tight here.
FeelingI am angry. I am upset. I am warm toward you.
StoryWhat I make it mean. About myself, about you, about what is happening.

Stories are not forbidden, they are simply the thing that carries us out of the present moment and out of contact with the feeling underneath. So we lean toward the sensations and emotions happening now.

Ways to say it in the room
Rather than the story about it, what is happening in you as you say it? Right now, in your body, what is here?

Own your experience, reveal it, share the impact

Honesty is what we are after

Owning means the feeling is mine. I do not put it on anyone else, I take it and I say it from inside: I feel something when you say that.

Then there is the movement outward. Noticing something inside is one thing. Speaking it is a step toward vulnerability, opening the experience into the space and sharing it with the others. That step is what we welcome most.

Sharing the impact is the other half: saying what it is like to be with what someone else just shared. It stays a report from my side, I feel X when you Y, never a verdict on the other person.

And a piece of wisdom worth saying out loud: sometimes someone shares something and you notice you are really annoyed, or that you badly want to save them. The practice is not to act on it and not to swallow it, but to notice it and voice it. I notice a strong wish to help you right now. That is the whole thing in one move.

Ways to say it in the room
I feel X when you Y. Hearing that, I notice… I notice a strong desire to rescue you right now, and I am staying with it instead.

Do not fix, change, or rescue

Sovereignty

The stance: honour each person's sovereignty and their capacity to hold their own state.

In practice: resist the urge to offer advice, to cheer someone up, to minimise discomfort, or to solve an emotion. Let people have their authentic experience without trying to make them comfortable or different.

This is the natural pair to the one above. The urge to fix is usually real and worth revealing. It is the acting on it that closes the space.

Ways to say it in the room
Nobody here needs to be fixed, and nothing here needs to be resolved by the end. You can be moved by someone without doing anything about it.

TwoWhat may be missing

You asked what the four might not cover. These are the candidates, and where each one probably belongs.

You choose how much to share Passing is a full answer. It is already promised in the event description, so it has to be said aloud. It is not really a principle, it is a permission, which makes it stronger, not weaker. Say it in the welcome, before the opening circle
Consent, and touch The greeting segment involves elbows, feet and backs. In a room where most people arrived alone, that needs its own framing: the invitation, the obvious way to decline, and a way to decline that costs nothing. Without it, the four principles quietly ask people to welcome something they never agreed to. Its own frame, right before the walking
What is said here stays here Never stated in the current four. One sentence, and people share differently for the rest of the evening. Welcome, with the practicalities
Curiosity instead of conclusion The present moment principle handles the story I tell about myself. It does not handle the story I tell about you. Checking a read out loud, you seem far away to me, is that true for you?, is a different move and it is most of what makes circling work. Either a fifth principle, or folded into pair round two
Slowness and silence Silence between exchanges is welcome, and slowness is what the whole evening is promising. Worth naming once so nobody rushes to fill gaps. One line inside the present moment principle
You can step out Leaving the room and coming back is allowed, and saying so early prevents one person from sitting through something they needed to leave. Welcome, with the practicalities

Arrival

Welcome and frame

Who the two of you are, a sentence each. Why you run this, something true rather than a pitch. What the shape of the evening is, in four beats, so nobody is guessing.

Opening circle

Dmitrijs

Everyone speaks once, sitting in the circle. Quite a bit of structure here, so nobody has to invent what to say:

  • Your name.
  • Three sensations you can feel in your body right now.
  • What you are arriving with, what it is like to be you at this moment, the mood.
  • What brought you, your curiosity or desire for today. Your intention.

Demonstrate it by going first, at the length you want back. Around a minute each.

Scaling. Twelve people at a minute is twelve minutes and it breathes. Seven is seven, and the minutes you save are worth giving to the closing circle, which needs them. Only above about sixteen does this round need cutting, by dropping to two sensations or splitting into two half circles with one of you in each.

Grounding meditation

Diana

Feeling the ground, then travelling through the body and letting each part relax. Fingernails, hands, feet, ankles, pelvis, chest, head.

Not a scan you rush. At each place the question is gentle and slow: what is it like to feel this part relaxing. Then staying with that a little.

This beat is the handover from talking to moving. Keep it soft rather than deep. The depth belongs later in the evening. The full script is written out at the end of this page.

Shaking

Walking and meeting

Diana, with the consent frame from Dmitrijs

Walking with yourself first. Then walking in the space, noticing there are other people in it. Then the invitation to greet someone physically, lightly: a greeting with the feet, with the elbows, whatever wants to happen. Feel what it is like to meet with an elbow.

Light and easy, seeing people, then letting it go and walking on. Several meetings, nothing held for long.

It closes standing back to back with one person, resting there, breathing.

Frame consent before this starts. The invitation, how to decline, and the fact that walking past someone is a complete and welcome answer. Thirty seconds of framing, and it changes how the whole segment feels for anyone who came alone.

Settling into sitting

Both

From back to back, sliding down to sitting with the same person. Stay back to back for a moment, then turn to face each other. This is the first pair, no partner picking required.

18:0515 min

Pairs, round one: staying with my experience

Dmitrijs

Facing your partner. The practice is the three layers, out loud.

  • 3 min. A speaks, B only listens. Present tense only: a sensation I notice… a feeling I notice… a thought I notice… Silence between is welcome.
  • 3 min. Swap.
  • 7 min. Ping pong. One line each, alternating. Right now I notice… Back and forth, no reply required, no conversation.

Demonstrate it, the two of you, for thirty seconds. Explaining takes three minutes and lands worse.

With an odd number, one of you partners up and the other keeps time.
18:2015 min

Break

Both

Tea, water, fruit. Not dead time: this is where people who came alone stop being alone. Both of you circulate toward whoever is standing on their own.

A fifteen minute break becomes twenty five without somebody calling people back. One of you owns the clock all evening, decide who before the doors open.
18:3515 min

Pairs, round two: sharing the impact

Dmitrijs

New partner. Same ping pong, one thing added: what happens in me when you say yours.

  • A: Right now I notice…
  • B: Hearing that, I notice… then their own.
  • Alternating, 12 minutes, with a switch of who opens halfway.

Two things to name before it starts. First, the impact is always from my side, I feel X when you Y, never a read on who they are. Second, if the urge to help or fix arrives, that is the material: say it instead of doing it.

If you want the curiosity principle in the evening, this is where it fits: add you seem [ ] to me, is that true for you? as a third permitted move.
18:5025 min

Threes

Both, moving between groups

Groups of three, counted off rather than self selected. One person is the focus, the other two are with them.

  • The focus person says what is alive for them right now. Not a story from the week, what is here.
  • The other two say what they notice in themselves while being with that person, and may ask what is here for them now.
  • No advice, no fixing, no going into the past.
  • Around seven minutes each, then a short pause and rotate.

This is the bridge into circling. Say that, so people know the last beat is not a new thing to learn.

Both of you float rather than sitting in one group. Ring a bell at each rotation so the groups stay together in time. Seven people makes one three and one four, and the four gets about five minutes each rather than seven. If only seven or eight arrive, staying in one circle for this beat is the better evening, and it turns the last two beats into one long circle.
19:1515 min

Full circle, free flowing, and close

Both

Everyone back into one circle. Open and unscripted: whoever is moved to speak, speaks. Same principles, no turns, no structure. One of you holds it lightly.

At around 19:24, bring it to the close: one sentence each, somatic. How am I right now, what am I taking with me. Model it short by going first. Then thank people, mention the next evening if there is one, and let the room stay for tea.

The tightest moment in the plan. At seven to twelve people the closing round costs four to six minutes, which leaves about ten for the open circle. Enough for a taste of it, not for a real one. If the free flowing circle is the part you most want, take five minutes from the threes and five from the principles and give this beat twenty five.

FiveThe grounding meditation, word for word

The 17:22 body scan written out in full. Spoken slowly, with pauses longer than feel natural. The words are a shape to speak from, not a text to recite.

Let yourself settle. Lying down or sitting, whatever your body wants right now. If closing the eyes feels good, let them close.

Feel the places where your body touches the ground. The ground is already holding you. There is nothing you have to do to earn that.

For the next few minutes there is nowhere to get to and nothing to achieve. We are going to travel through the body, slowly, and simply visit each part. Whatever you find there is welcome: warmth, tension, tingling, heaviness, or nothing much at all. Nothing much is also an answer. Nothing needs to be fixed, and nothing needs to leave.

Begin with the feet. The heels resting on the floor, the soles, each toe. Notice whatever is there right now. And ask, softly: what is it like to let the feet relax? Stay with that a moment.

Let your attention travel up into the ankles, the calves, the knees. The legs have carried you all day, and now they get to do nothing at all. If somewhere feels tight, you do not have to change it. Let your attention rest there, warm and unhurried.

Come up into the thighs, and into the pelvis. Let the whole weight of the pelvis sink toward the ground. This is the base of the body, and it can be heavy here, fully supported.

The belly. Let it be soft. Let the breath move it however it wants. If a feeling lives here right now, restlessness, calm, something without a name, it can stay. Give it room, and keep breathing.

The chest, and the heart. Notice what the weather is like in there at this moment. Whatever you meet, it does not need to be different. See if you can stay beside it the way you would sit beside a friend: not fixing anything, just present, just company.

The shoulders. Sometimes the whole day is held here. Invite them to soften toward the ground, and if they soften only a little, that is enough.

Let the attention pour down the arms, through the elbows and the wrists, into the hands. The palms, each finger, all the way out to the fingernails. Even the fingernails get a moment of attention tonight. What is it like to feel the hands completely at rest?

The neck, and the throat. The jaw: let it unclench, let the teeth part slightly. The face: the cheeks, the small muscles around the eyes, the forehead. The scalp. The whole head, resting.

Now feel the body as one whole, from the heels to the top of the head, here, breathing by itself. You are in this room, in this moment, and nothing more is asked of you.

A long pause here, up to a minute if the room is settled

When you are ready, let the breath grow a little deeper. Small movements in the fingers and the toes. Take your time, and in your own time, let the eyes open.
