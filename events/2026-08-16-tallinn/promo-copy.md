## Ad copy

Facebook cuts the primary text at roughly 125 characters, so the first line has
to carry the whole ad on its own. The opening question is the same one the event
description opens with, which keeps the ad and the landing page consistent.

**Final, 1 August.** Dmitrijs wrote the body; this is that text cleaned up.

> How do you stay open to another person, without abandoning yourself?
>
> An evening of circling and authentic relating: slowing down, saying what's
> true, and meeting the people in the room properly. An open space for
> fulfilling connection, in the truth of the moment.
>
> Sunday 16 August, 16:30. Üks Maja, Tallinn. Held in English, no experience
> needed. 🌊

Three things were changed and each one has a reason.

- **"meeting in aliveness" is out.** It is a word this community uses and a
  cold Tallinn scroller does not, and this ad is going almost entirely to
  people who have never heard of circling.
- **"relating to the people in the room" became "meeting the people in the
  room properly".** The sentence already says "authentic relating" eight words
  earlier, so the repeat was doing nothing.
- **"held in English, no experience needed" was added.** It answers the one
  objection this audience actually has, and it costs a single line.

"Fulfilling connection" and "the truth of the moment" are Dmitrijs's own and
they stay. The first line is 68 characters, comfortably inside the cut.

Short, for a story or a second placement:

> How do you stay open to another person, without abandoning yourself? An
> evening of circling and authentic relating. Sunday 16 August, Tallinn.

Headline: `Circling & Authentic Relating` · Description: `Sunday 16 August,
16:30 · Üks Maja · In English` · Button: `Get tickets`.

**Only run one ad text.** At €60 there are roughly eighty clicks in total.
That is enough to tell two or three images apart and nowhere near enough to
tell two texts apart, so the images are the variable and the text is fixed.

Every background plate in `brand/bg/` is landscape, so the obvious way to build
a story is to drop the art into a band in the middle. Don't: it reads as a
landscape picture parked in a vertical frame. These scale the plate past the
frame width and anchor it to the very top, so it bleeds off three edges and
dissolves into the ground colour on the fourth. It costs some cropping at the
sides, which is the right trade.
